Presentation Title
Inside UC Berkeley Admissions: How Excellence, Context, and Impact Come Together

Global Style Direction
Create a premium, cinematic, editorial-quality presentation that demystifies the UC Berkeley admissions process for prospective applicants, families, counselors, and educators. The deck should feel like a blend of an Apple education keynote, a Netflix documentary about ambition and opportunity, and a high-end university brand campaign. The goal is to visually teach how Berkeley evaluates applicants while creating an emotionally immersive journey through academic excellence, personal context, intellectual curiosity, public mission, and community impact.

This is not an official UC Berkeley admissions document and must not claim access to confidential admissions data or insider-only information. Present the content as a thoughtful, transparent, research-informed overview based on publicly available admissions principles, UC application guidance, and Berkeley’s public academic culture. Avoid making guarantees, formulas, or oversimplified claims such as “do X to get admitted.” Emphasize holistic review, context, fit, authenticity, academic preparation, and contribution to campus community.

Suggested slide count: 14 slides.

Core Artistic Direction
Design the presentation as an immersive journey from the outside gates of Berkeley into the inner logic of holistic admissions. Use cinematic campus-inspired visuals: golden-hour shots of Sather Gate, aerial views of the Campanile, student silhouettes walking through eucalyptus-lined paths, close-up editorial imagery of notebooks, laptops, lab benches, protest posters, library interiors, and Bay Area light. Combine these with elegant information design: translucent admission-review layers, application-flow diagrams, rubric-style evaluation cards, timeline roadmaps, and decision-context maps.

The deck should feel intellectually rigorous but emotionally human. Use visual metaphors: admissions as a layered lens, a mosaic of evidence, a constellation of choices, a bridge between preparation and possibility, and a campus ecosystem where students contribute to research, activism, innovation, and service.

Color Palette
Use a refined UC Berkeley-inspired palette without becoming overly literal or generic:
- Berkeley Midnight Navy: #003262 as the primary foundation color
- California Gold: #FDB515 for emphasis, highlights, lines, icons, and key callouts
- Fog White: #F5F1E8 for warm backgrounds and editorial pages
- Bay Gradient Blue: #0A4C8A to #1C7ED6 for depth and digital overlays
- Eucalyptus Green: #4E7A5B for context, growth, and personal-development sections
- Graphite Black: #111111 for premium contrast
- Soft Silver: #D8DDE3 for cards, dividers, and subtle interface lines
- Warm Terracotta: #C66A3D as a secondary accent for human-story moments

Use deep navy backgrounds for authority, warm cream pages for human storytelling, and gold accent strokes to create a sense of prestige and focus. Avoid childish school colors; make it refined, cinematic, and editorial.

Typography Direction
Use a modern, academic-meets-tech typography system:
- Main headline font: elegant serif or high-contrast editorial typeface such as Canela, Playfair Display, or a similar premium serif
- Secondary headline and body font: clean modern sans serif such as Inter, Neue Haas Grotesk, SF Pro, or Helvetica Now
- Data labels and diagram text: compact sans serif with strong readability, such as IBM Plex Sans or Inter

Typography should feel spacious, confident, and cinematic. Use large, restrained headlines with generous negative space. Use short, precise text blocks. Avoid dense paragraphs. Pair oversized chapter numerals with small explanatory subtitles. Use gold micro-labels such as “HOLISTIC REVIEW,” “CONTEXT,” “ACADEMIC PREPARATION,” and “PERSONAL INSIGHT.”

Layout System
Use a premium editorial-grid system with strong hierarchy:
- 16:9 widescreen format
- 12-column grid for text and data pages
- Asymmetric compositions with large image fields and minimal text
- Layered glassmorphism panels for admissions-process diagrams
- Vertical timeline layouts for application milestones
- Split-screen layouts contrasting “What applicants submit” vs. “What reviewers evaluate”
- Modular KPI-style cards for admissions factors
- Large cinematic hero imagery with translucent navy overlays and gold linework

Every slide should feel uniquely composed, not templated. Maintain consistency through color, typography, line weight, icon style, and cinematic lighting.

Animation Style
Use subtle, elegant keynote-style motion:
- Slow cinematic fades and cross-dissolves between campus imagery
- Gold line drawings animating as if being sketched over photos
- Layered review lenses sliding into place one by one
- Diagram nodes gently glowing as they are introduced
- Timeline milestones appearing with soft parallax movement
- Cards floating in with slight depth and shadow
- Data charts animating smoothly from baseline with restrained easing
- Avoid gimmicky transitions, spinning objects, or cartoon effects

The motion should feel polished, calm, and intentional, like a premium documentary interface.

Visual Identity
The visual identity should communicate academic prestige, intellectual curiosity, and social impact. Combine:
- Cinematic Berkeley campus photography
- Editorial documentary portraits of diverse students studying, researching, organizing, creating, and collaborating
- UI-style overlays representing application review
- Gold vector lines, thin dividers, small coordinates, and map-inspired marks
- Subtle textures: paper grain, archival documents, campus stone, notebook pages, glass reflections
- Futuristic but restrained dashboards for admissions process explanation

Use imagery that reflects Berkeley’s identity: rigorous academics, research culture, activism, public service, entrepreneurship, interdisciplinary exploration, and California context.

Diagram & Chart Style
Create diagrams that visually teach admissions concepts rather than merely decorate the slides. Use:
- Layered holistic review model: academics, context, achievements, essays, activities, major readiness, contribution
- Application anatomy diagram showing UC application components
- Timeline roadmap from course planning to application submission to decisions
- Context lens diagram showing how opportunity, school environment, responsibilities, and lived experience shape evaluation
- Major-readiness map showing academic preparation by intended field
- Personal Insight Questions matrix showing evidence, reflection, specificity, and voice
- Activity-impact pyramid showing participation, initiative, leadership, and measurable contribution
- Myth-vs-reality comparison cards
- Final applicant strategy canvas

Use thin gold lines, translucent navy glass panels, clean labels, elegant iconography, and layered depth. Keep text concise and visual.
